# flui research brief: how to train Spanish speakers to actually use better words

## 1. Executive summary: the principles to build on

1. **Train active use, not recognition.** Adults already recognize far more words than they use. The hardest step is producing the word from its meaning (form recall), so "Ya es tuya" has to require that step. A 3-option cloze only trains recognition. *(High confidence: Laufer & Goldstein 2004.)*
2. **A word needs many varied encounters.** Plan for about 10–12 across different contexts. One "palabra del día" post is about one encounter, which is far too few. *(Medium: McKeown et al. 1985; Webb 2007.)*
3. **Relearning spread over days matters more than drilling on day one.** Good default: reach 1–2 correct recalls in the first session, then 3 successful recalls on separate days. *(High: Rawson & Dunlosky 2011; 2022.)*
4. **Keep "pista, no respuesta", but cap it.** With 3 options you can only be wrong twice. Score only the first try. Always explain why each wrong option fails. *(High: Butler & Roediger 2008; Little & Bjork 2015.)*
5. **Look-alike and near-synonym words work well as distractors but badly as new words taught together.** Never introduce "perspicaz" and "perspicuo" as new words in the same week. *(Medium-high: Nation 2000; Tinkham 1993.)*
6. **Choose words that replace something the user already says** ("tema", "cosa", "hacer", "muy bueno"). They should be mid-frequency, shared across Spanish-speaking countries, and low risk of sounding stuck-up. Avoid the rare literary words that dominate social media ("solaz", "inexorable").
7. **Due reviews come first; new words fill what's left of the time.** The rule "10 min = 1 word" only holds while no reviews are piling up.
8. **Use forgiving habit mechanics.** One missed day does not break habit formation, and a broken streak makes people stop. Use weekly consistency plus a free streak repair, and never guilt notifications.
9. **Tone is "you'll sound like yourself, only sharper", never "don't say nonsense".** Regional usages are variation, not errors, and you should say so explicitly.
10. **Write every definition in-house.** RAE forbids commercial reproduction or extraction of its content. Use the DPD and DLE only as references you check against, never as a data source.

## 3. From learning science to product rules

| # | Evidence | Product rule | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Knowing a word has a difficulty ladder: recognizing the meaning < recognizing the form < recalling the meaning < **recalling the form** (Laufer & Goldstein 2004). Nation splits word knowledge into form, meaning and use, each receptive and productive. | A 3-option cloze trains recognition only. "Ya es tuya" requires at least one **form recall**: type or say the word from a context or meaning. It also requires one **use judgment**: "¿Está bien usada aquí?" | High |
| 2 | 12 encounters beat 4 on every measure, and rich instruction (varied contexts, active use) beats definitions (McKeown, Beck, Omanson & Pople 1985, children). Webb 2007 (L2) found gains up to 10 encounters, and even then meaning recall was only about 29%. | Design about 12 meaningful encounters per word before mastery. The first session gives about 7 (explanation, example, 2 clozes, 3 readings) and reviews add 4–6. Every cloze sentence must be new, so users learn the word rather than memorize the sentence. | Medium (evidence is from children and second-language learners) |
| 3 | Successive relearning: recall 3 times at first, then relearn 3 times at widely spaced intervals. The benefit of a stricter first session fades once relearning happens, and relearning is cheap: under 2 minutes per item at first, under 1 minute later (Rawson & Dunlosky 2011, 2022). | End the first session with one **unaided** correct retrieval in a fresh sentence. Mastery requires **3 first-try successes in separate sessions on 3 or more different days**. | High |
| 4 | The best gap between reviews is about 20–40% of how long you want to remember for a 1-week horizon, falling to 5–10% for 1 year (Cepeda et al. 2008). FSRS models each item's difficulty, stability and chance of recall; at the same retention it needs about 20–30% fewer reviews than SM-2. | Start new users on a fixed ladder: day 1, 3, 7, 14, 30. Switch to FSRS (open-source ts-fsrs or py-fsrs) at a desired retention of 0.85–0.90 once there is data. Score answers: first-try correct = Good, second try = Hard, third try or revealed = Again. | High on spacing, medium on the exact parameters |
| 5 | Using the word in your own sentence (strong evaluation) beats fill-in-the-blank, which beats reading (Hulstijn & Laufer 2001). When time on task is equal the gap narrows (Webb 2005). | Every word gets at least one production task ("Úsala") before mastery. Readings alone don't count toward mastery. | Medium-high |
| 6 | Trying to answer before seeing the answer improves later learning, even when the attempt fails (Kornell, Hays & Bjork 2009). High-confidence errors are corrected best, because the surprise gets attention (Butterfield & Metcalfe 2001). | The pista-first flow is supported. Optionally add a one-tap confidence check ("¿Segura?") on reviews so confident errors get richer feedback. | High |
| 7 | Multiple-choice lures can be learned as if they were true (Roediger & Marsh 2005); feedback largely neutralizes this (Butler & Roediger 2008). Competitive distractors also strengthen what users learn about the wrong options (Little & Bjork 2015). Three options are psychometrically optimal (Rodriguez 2005). | **3 options is correct.** The final explanation must say **why each wrong option fails**, not just confirm the right one. Distractors must be plausible but clearly wrong in that context: exactly one right answer. | High |
| 8 | Learning semantically related or look-alike words at the same time causes interference: about 50% more trials needed (Tinkham 1993; Nation 2000). Poorly established words suffer most. | Never introduce paronyms or near-synonyms as **new** words within about 7 days of each other. A "contraste" exercise (perspicaz vs. perspicuo) unlocks only after one of the pair reaches Practica with at least 2 successes. Mix reviews across topics. | Medium-high |
| 9 | Hints get abused: learners click through to the final giveaway hint, and that behavior predicts lower learning (Aleven et al. 2006, 2016; Baker et al. 2004). Feedback that is too long gets ignored (Shute 2008). | Hints are **response-specific** (target the option chosen) and under about 25 words. Guessed or hint-assisted answers earn no reward. Scheduling uses first-try accuracy only. See the hint policy below. | High |
| 10 | Gamification has small-to-moderate effects on learning (g = 0.49), motivation (0.36) and behavior (0.25), less stable in rigorous studies (Sailer & Homner 2020). | Gamification is seasoning, not the engine. | Medium |

### Hint and retry policy
1. **Attempt 1 wrong → Pista 1.** A context cue about meaning that fits the distractor chosen. No spelling cues.
2. **Attempt 2 wrong → Pista 2.** A contrast: what the chosen word really means, and why it doesn't fit.
3. **Third choice is forced.** Reveal it with a short celebration ("¡Ahí está!") and the full explanation. Count it as **Again**.
4. **Re-queue the item** in a new sentence at the end of the session. The word can't leave today's session without one unaided success. If that also fails, schedule it for tomorrow; don't loop more than once.
5. **Frustration cap.** After 3 forced reveals in one session, switch to an easier mode (reading or a recognition review) and show "Hoy estás sembrando; mañana cosechas."
6. **Free-text recall** ("Úsala"): accept inflections and minor typos, give up to 2 hints (the second may be the first letter), then reveal.

### Microlearning and habit
- **One new word per activity block.** Limited new information per screen.
- **Missing one day doesn't matter.** In Lally et al. (2010), a single missed day did not affect habit formation; median time to automaticity was 66 days, ranging 18–254. A 2024 meta-analysis found medians of 59–66 days, with morning and self-chosen habits stronger (Singh et al. 2024).
  - Implication: onboard with "¿A qué hora lo harás?" and use a 60-day framing, not "21 días".
- **Streaks work, but only if they're forgiving.** Duolingo found learners on a 7-day streak are 2.4× more likely to return the next day. Letting one lesson extend the streak, instead of the full daily goal, raised the share on 7+ day streaks by over 40%. Broken streaks demotivate, especially when users blame themselves, and a repair option softens that (Silverman & Barasch 2023).
  - Rule: extend the streak with **any** completed activity, including a 3-minute review. Give a free weekly repair. Show "5 de 7 días esta semana" instead of a fragile counter.

## 4. Content model

### Fields for a word entry
- **Identity:** lemma, part of speech, gender and number forms, word family (e.g. perspicacia, perspicazmente).
- **Pronunciation:** syllables, stressed syllable, IPA, and audio in **two voices** where pronunciation differs (seseo vs. distinción).
- **Explicación amigable:** written in-house, no more than 20 words, using only common words (Beck's "student-friendly explanation").
- **Register and risk:** neutral / culto / coloquial, plus a 1–3 score for the risk of sounding pedantic, plus "cuándo NO usarla".
- **Frequency:** per-million frequency and source (Leipzig or OpenSubtitles lists are openly licensed); tier.
- **Regional notes:** per-country variants and warnings.
- **Collocations and grammar limits:** what it combines with and what it can describe.
- **`reemplaza`:** the vague or comodín expressions it replaces, as before/after pairs. This is the heart of the product.
- **`confusiones`:** paronyms and near-synonyms, each with a one-line difference and a memory trick.
- **Exercise bank:** at least 4 cloze items. Each item has one right answer, 2 distractors typed (paronym / meaning neighbor / wrong register), a "why not" line per distractor, Pista 1, Pista 2 per distractor, and a final explanation.
- **Contexts:** at least 3 scenes, tagged by domain (work, social, interview, family) and by Duhigg's conversation type.
- **Production prompts:** situation, task, and a rubric (right sense, grammar, natural register).
- **Editorial metadata:** author, reviewer, sources consulted (not copied), licence, review date.

### Word selection criteria (all must pass)
1. **Useful** in 3 or more everyday areas of life.
2. **Replaces** a frequent vague word, comodín or filler.
3. **Mid-frequency:** users probably recognize it but don't use it.
4. **Pan-Hispanic,** or its regional variants are documented.
5. **Risk of sounding pedantic ≤ 2.**
6. **Explainable with common words.**

Favor verbs that replace "hacer/poner/tener" (plantear, abordar, matizar, sopesar) and connectors that replace fillers (es decir, en concreto, dicho de otro modo).

### Spanish error domains to build packs from
- **Dequeísmo / queísmo.** Use the DPD's question test: *¿Qué piensas?* → *Pienso que…*; *¿De qué te alegras?* → *Me alegro de que…*. Note the DPD says queísmo is less stigmatized.
- **Comodines:** cosa, tema, hacer, poner, realizar, bueno. For example, "realizar la comida" is a misuse.
- **Fillers by region:** este (MX), en plan (ES), tipo (AR), o sea (everywhere).
- **Redundancies:** "subir arriba", "lapso de tiempo".
- **Paronyms:** actitud/aptitud, infligir/infringir, prever/proveer, perjuicio/prejuicio.
- **Unnecessary anglicisms:** following Lázaro Carreter's categories.

### Worked entry: **perspicaz**
- **Categoría:** adjetivo, igual en masculino y femenino; plural *perspicaces*. Familia: *perspicacia*, *perspicazmente*.
- **Pronunciación:** pers-pi-**CAZ** (aguda). Latinoamérica, Canarias y Andalucía occidental: [pers.piˈkas]; centro y norte de España: [pers.piˈkaθ].
- **Explicación:** "Que se da cuenta rápido de lo que no es obvio: capta detalles, intenciones o problemas que otros no ven."
- **Registro:** neutral-culto; natural en el trabajo y entre adultos. Riesgo de sonar pedante: 1.
- **Consejo de uso:** suena mejor como elogio a otra persona ("qué observación tan perspicaz") que para describirte a ti ("soy muy perspicaz" suena a presumir).
- **Reemplaza:** "muy listo/a", "se da cuenta de todo", "tiene buen ojo", "muy avispado".
- **Colocaciones:** observación / pregunta / mirada / comentario perspicaz; "ser perspicaz para los negocios".
- **Confusiones:**
  - *Suspicaz*: quien tiende a sospechar (connotación negativa).
  - *Perspicuo*: claro, fácil de entender; se dice de un texto o de quien se explica con claridad. Viene de la misma raíz latina *perspicere*, 'mirar a través'.
  - **Truco:** el perspicaz *ve*; lo perspicuo *se ve*.
- **Cuándo NO usarla:** para "desconfiado" (usa *suspicaz*) ni para "claro" (usa *claro* o *perspicuo*).

**Cloze exercise:**
> Andrés confía plenamente en su equipo, pero es tan ______ que detectó en dos minutos el error de las cifras que nadie había visto.
> **a) suspicaz · b) perspicaz ✓ · c) perspicuo**

The first clause rules out *suspicaz*, and "detectó" rules out *perspicuo*, so only one answer works.

- **Pista 1** (general, en ambos casos): "La palabra describe a alguien que capta rápido lo que no es evidente."
- **Pista 2** (según lo que eligió):
  - Si eligió *suspicaz*: "'Suspicaz' es quien sospecha de los demás, y Andrés confía en su equipo."
  - Si eligió *perspicuo*: "'Perspicuo' se dice de lo que se entiende con claridad, como una explicación. Andrés no explica: descubre."
- **Explicación final:** "¡Eso es! *Perspicaz*: que se da cuenta rápido de lo que no es obvio. *Suspicaz* se parece, pero es quien desconfía. *Perspicuo* comparte raíz, pero describe lo que se entiende fácilmente. El perspicaz ve; lo perspicuo se ve."

**En contexto:**
1. **Reunión de trabajo (conversación práctica).** "Mientras todos discutían el calendario, Carla preguntó: '¿Y quién aprueba los cambios si el cliente no responde?'. Silencio. Nadie lo había pensado. El director sonrió: 'Muy perspicaz, Carla. Eso nos habría retrasado un mes'." *Antes decías:* "¡Qué buena pregunta, qué lista!" → *Ahora:* "Qué pregunta tan perspicaz."
2. **Café con una amiga (conversación emocional).** "Sofía escuchaba a Marta hablar de su nuevo trabajo con entusiasmo, pero notó que nunca mencionaba a su jefe. 'Oye, ¿todo bien con él?'. Marta suspiró. 'Eres muy perspicaz… la verdad, no'." *Antes:* "Te das cuenta de todo." → *Ahora:* "Eres muy perspicaz."
3. **Entrevista (conversación social).** "Al final, Diego preguntó cómo medían el éxito en los primeros tres meses. La entrevistadora anotó algo y dijo: 'Es una pregunta perspicaz; casi nadie la hace'." *Nota:* aquí el elogio lo recibe él; no hace falta que se describa a sí mismo como perspicaz.

**Úsala:** "Tu compañero notó que el cliente estaba molesto antes de que dijera nada. Díselo usando *perspicaz*." Rubric: the word is used as praise of a person or observation, and the sentence sounds natural.

## 5. Session design: refining "10 min = 1 word"

These are working estimates; instrument them early.

- **A full new-word loop** (Descubre → Entiende → Mira → 2 clozes → 3 readings → Úsala → final cloze) takes about **6–8 minutes**.
- **A review item** takes about **20–45 seconds**, since relearning is fast (Rawson & Dunlosky).
- **Review load:** with the 1-3-7-14-30 ladder, each new word creates **about 5 reviews in its first month**.
  - Steady state at 1 new word per day: about 5 reviews per day, roughly 3 minutes.
  - At 3 per day: about 15 reviews per day, roughly 8–10 minutes.
  - So someone who picks 30 minutes for a week and then drops to 10 will drown in reviews.

**Algorithm:** `new_words = min(floor((budget − due_reviews_time) / 7 min), cap)`.
- Cap: 3 new words per day, and no new paronym pairs within 7 days.
- If due reviews take more than 50% of the budget, show "Hoy toca afianzar" with 0–1 new words.

| Time chosen | Plan |
|---|---|
| 5 min | Reviews only. On day 1, a "light" word: explanation + 1 cloze + 1 reading, finished tomorrow. |
| 10 min | Due reviews (≤ 3 min) + 1 new word |
| 20 min | Reviews (≤ 5 min) + 2 new words, separated by a review block |
| 30 min | Reviews + up to 3 new words + 1 "contraste" or "Habla" task |

**Session order:** warm-up review of 1–2 easy items (quick win), then due reviews mixed across topics, then new words, then an end-of-session check using new sentences for today's words, then the "úsala hoy" challenge.

**Asking the time daily:** keep the question, since it supports adult autonomy, but preselect yesterday's choice so it's a single tap.

## 6. Mastery state machine

- **Nueva → Practica.** The user finished the discovery flow and got one unaided correct answer at the end of the first session (the in-session criterion from §3).
- **Practica → Ya es tuya.** All of the following:
  - First-try successes in **3 or more review sessions on 3 or more distinct days, spanning 7 or more days**.
  - At least **1 form recall** (typed or spoken).
  - At least **1 success in a sentence the user hasn't seen before**.
  - At least **1 production task** accepted by the rubric.
  - The most recent review was first-try correct.
  - Once FSRS is active: stability of 21 days or more.

  *(Medium confidence. Each part is well supported; the combination is our own design. Validate it with a delayed recall test at 30 days.)*
- **Ya es tuya → Practica.** A review goes wrong (Again). Message: "Vamos a refrescarla". Keep the word's history; never reset it to Nueva.
- **Hidden sub-states for scheduling:** due, overdue, forced reveal.
- **Real-world use** ("¿La usaste hoy?", Toastmasters-style) is shown as a badge. It never gates mastery, because it is self-reported.

## 7. Competitive gaps and differentiation

| Product | Strength | Weakness vs. flui |
|---|---|---|
| **Vocabulary – Learn words daily** (Monkey Taps; 5M+ Android installs, has a Spanish version), **Palabra del día – Diccionario** | Low-friction swiping, widgets | Passive exposure; no retrieval, no production; rare-word bias; ads and paywalls; users report a broken widget |
| **Elevate** | Polished games, "Words of the Day" | **English only** |
| **Duolingo** (streaks; Max: Roleplay, Video Call) | Habit engineering, AI conversation | For learning a foreign language; criticized for gamification misuse (Hadi Mogavi et al. 2022) |
| **Babbel Speak** (Sept 2025) | Scaffolded AI speaking with hints | Foreign-language beginners only |
| **Orai** (English only), **Speeko** (Apple, English), **Yoodli** (supports Spanish; team/enterprise focus) | Delivery metrics: fillers, pace | They measure *how* you speak, not *which words* you choose. Filler detection tuned for English can misscore Spanish |
| **Creators** (Jurado, Barceló, Garay) | Aspiration, reach, trust | No practice loop, no spacing, no feedback |
| **RAE DLE app / FundéuRAE** | Authority | Reference tools, not training |

**What flui can own** (I found no Spanish-native product doing this):
- Productive vocabulary training for **native adult** Spanish speakers.
- Combined in one loop: "reemplaza X" framing, retrieval with pistas, contrast between look-alike words, scenes tagged by conversation type, and pan-Hispanic sensitivity.
- Later, "Habla" can score **word choice** in Spanish, which no speech coach does today.

## 8. Risks and anti-patterns

- **Pedantry.** Rare-word showcases contradict "habla como quieres sonar". Enforce the pedantry score and the "cuándo NO usarla" field.
- **Shaming prescriptivism.** Present regional usages as variation, and tone down corrections the DPD itself treats as less stigmatized, such as queísmo. Copy should never say "disparate" to the user.
- **False learning from lures.** Always explain every distractor. Never leave a guessed answer unexplained.
- **Gaming the hints.** With 3 options users can brute-force. Rewards and scheduling must use first-try accuracy only.
- **Dark patterns to avoid:** guilt notifications (Duolingo's "you made Duo sad"-style messages), paid streak repair, leaderboards that shame, and income or "99%" claims (a consumer-protection risk too).
- **Licensing:**
  - **RAE** expressly prohibits reproducing, transforming or **extracting and reusing** its website content for commercial purposes without prior written authorization. So: no scraping or paraphrase-mirroring the DLE; write original definitions.
  - **FundéuRAE's** reuse terms could not be verified. Assume all rights reserved and ask for permission or a partnership.
  - **Wiktionary data** (CC BY-SA) is **ShareAlike**, which can force derived content onto the same licence. Keep it out of the core content, or isolate it.
  - **Frequency lists:** Leipzig lists are CC BY-4.0. CORPES and CREA lists are downloadable for research but carry no open licence.
  - **Creators:** don't copy their word lists or scripts, and avoid the "#SinMuletillas®" name.
- **AI-generated content.** It will invent meanings and regional claims. Require a human linguistic review before anything ships.
- **Validity gap.** Most exposure-count evidence comes from children or second-language learners. Run a 30-day delayed productive test on a cohort to calibrate the mastery gates.
